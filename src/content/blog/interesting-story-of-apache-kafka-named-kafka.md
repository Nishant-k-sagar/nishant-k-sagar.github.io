## Humans and Naming complexities

When humans build something, one of the most interesting and meaningful stages is naming it.
In the software industry, it is even more fascinating than in many other industries, as there are plenty of examples where projects are named after historical context, literature, personal experiences, or random things rather than some underlying technology or process.

## Story behind Kafka naming
One such interesting case is Apache Kafka.
Jay Kreps, one of Kafka's creators, had taken several literature classes in college and was impressed by the works of Franz Kafka. He was also a fan of his writing.

Now, here's the interesting part.
Kafka was built as a distributed messaging system, with an append-only commit log at its core, designed for high-throughput data ingestion.

Kreps thought that since the system was optimized for writing, naming it after a writer would make sense. He already liked Franz Kafka's work and thought the name was short, distinct, and cool for an open-source project.
And that's pretty much how Kafka got its name!

Interestingly, there wasn't any deeper technical connection between Franz Kafka's literary works and the software itself, as Kreps later clarified.

## Few other cases mentioned
Naming a project will always be a story in itself.
Be it Debian, Python, Jira, Hadoop, Java, Laravel, and many more. Some have historical connections, some have personal stories, and some have completely unexpected origins.

Such fun stories! Love the fun element and build ups in software systmes.
